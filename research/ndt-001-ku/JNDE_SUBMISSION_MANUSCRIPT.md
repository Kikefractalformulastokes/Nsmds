# Evidence-First Assurance of Ultrasonic Guided-Wave Damage Localization: Blind Re-evaluation, Tail Risk, Distribution Shift, and Claim Control

**Author:** Enrique Sánchez Lorenzo  
**Affiliation:** CoreSyn — Independent Evidence & Model Assurance Lab

## Abstract
Data-driven non-destructive testing (NDT) can report strong average localization accuracy while concealing instability, distribution-shift sensitivity, and rare but consequential errors. We present an evidence-first assurance study using a public ultrasonic guided-wave dataset for delamination localization in a cross-ply composite beam. The analysis freezes source files by cryptographic digest, establishes a nearest-neighbour reference, evaluates generic continuous localization without post-hoc test tuning, performs paired uncertainty analysis and ablation, and then re-evaluates the frozen challenger on a deterministic internal blind subset. On the original noise-free alternating-position holdout (n=40), nearest-neighbour localization produced 15.000 mm mean absolute error (MAE), whereas inverse-square two-neighbour continuous interpolation produced 8.668 mm MAE, an observed 42.21% reduction. However, the paired bootstrap 95% interval for mean improvement was [-3.351, 13.978] mm and the challenger exhibited a 108.315 mm maximum error. Under a corrected deterministic internal blind re-split (n=16), the same generic challenger achieved 14.403 mm MAE versus 15.000 mm for the reference, only a 3.98% observed reduction, while retaining a 102.083 mm maximum error. Under the studied mixed-noise OOD condition, the generic continuous method also degraded relative to the nearest-neighbour reference. Ablation showed that the observed holdout improvement is attributable to generic continuous interpolation rather than to any canonical NS-MDS mechanism. These results illustrate why average benchmark improvement alone is insufficient evidence for operational NDT claims. We propose a reproducible assurance chain combining provenance, frozen evaluation, paired uncertainty, tail-risk reporting, ablation, blind re-evaluation, OOD stress testing, and explicit claim gates.

**Keywords:** non-destructive testing; ultrasonic guided waves; structural health monitoring; damage localization; model assurance; out-of-distribution; reproducibility; uncertainty; composite structures

## 1. Introduction
Ultrasonic guided waves are attractive for structural health monitoring because they can interrogate comparatively large structural regions from sparse sensing. Increasingly, data-driven localization methods are evaluated on simulated or experimental waveform datasets. Yet a low aggregate error on a benchmark does not by itself establish robustness, transferability, causal attribution, or fitness for operational inspection.

This distinction matters in safety-relevant NDT. A model may improve mean error while creating a small number of large localization failures. A result may also depend on a particular split, preprocessing choice, interpolation rule, or distribution regime. If these factors are not separated, an exploratory improvement can be overstated as evidence of a deployable system.

This work therefore asks a different question from conventional leaderboard evaluation: what can the available evidence actually support? We construct a staged assurance chain around a public ultrasonic guided-wave delamination dataset. The chain is intentionally designed to expose rather than hide uncertainty: source provenance is frozen; a simple reference is retained; candidate improvements are evaluated without test-label tuning; paired uncertainty and tail behavior are reported; ablation identifies the source of apparent lift; OOD behavior is inspected; and a deterministic internal blind re-split tests whether the headline effect persists under a changed evaluation partition.

## 2. Data and provenance
We use the public dataset “Multiple ultrasonic-guided wave signals generated by wave propagation model: focused on delamination damage location variations in cross-ply composite beams,” released by Lu et al. through KU Leuven RDR (DOI: 10.48804/JAIG58). The dataset documentation describes transient finite-element guided-wave simulations for a cross-ply carbon-fibre composite beam with a 5 mm delamination. The assurance run pins the source CSVs by SHA-256 and consistently maps normalized damage position p to physical location as x_mm = 300 + 400p.

## 3. Methods
The primary noise-free evaluation assigns alternating damage positions to training and holdout sets. A transparent nearest-neighbour predictor in raw waveform space is retained as reference. The principal challenger selects the two nearest training waveforms and estimates continuous location using inverse-square distance weighting. This is a generic interpolation baseline and is not represented as canonical NS-MDS.

Frozen methods are also evaluated on supplied averaged 10 dB in-distribution data and mixed-noise out-of-distribution data. For the primary n=40 holdout, per-sample absolute errors are paired and evaluated using a 20,000-replicate paired bootstrap with seed 20260827. Ablation compares nearest neighbour, uniform two-neighbour interpolation, inverse-distance two-neighbour interpolation, inverse-square two-neighbour interpolation, and a three-neighbour inverse-square variant.

A deterministic 20% subset of the 81 noise-free locations is held out using seed 20260827 before prediction (n=16), with the remaining 65 samples used for training. This is an internal blind re-split, not independent external validation, aircraft testing, or third-party validation.

## 4. Results
On the n=40 noise-free alternating-position holdout, nearest-neighbour achieved 15.000 mm MAE and inverse-square two-neighbour interpolation achieved 8.668 mm MAE, an observed 6.332 mm mean improvement and 42.21% relative reduction. The challenger was better on 36/40 samples and worse on 4/40. Median error was 0.247 mm and P90 4.608 mm, but maximum error reached 108.315 mm.

The 20,000-replicate paired bootstrap interval for mean improvement was [-3.351, 13.978] mm. Because the 95% interval crosses zero, the experiment does not support a conventional 95%-confidence superiority claim.

Ablation produced primary-holdout MAEs of 15.000 mm for nearest neighbour, 10.000 mm for uniform two-neighbour interpolation, 9.287 mm for inverse-distance two-neighbour interpolation, 8.668 mm for inverse-square two-neighbour interpolation, and 43.904 mm for inverse-square three-neighbour interpolation. This pattern attributes the apparent lift to generic two-neighbour continuous interpolation rather than canonical NS-MDS.

On averaged 10 dB ID data, nearest-neighbour MAE was 7.407 mm and the two-neighbour continuous method achieved 4.637 mm. On mixed-noise OOD data, nearest-neighbour MAE was 6.667 mm whereas the continuous method degraded to 19.575 mm, with maximum error 103.540 mm.

On the corrected deterministic blind subset, nearest-neighbour again produced 15.000 mm MAE while the frozen challenger produced 14.403 mm MAE, an observed 3.98% relative reduction. It was better on 12/16 cases and worse on 4/16. Median challenger error was 0.406 mm, P90 49.383 mm, and maximum error 102.083 mm.

## 5. Discussion
The principal finding is not universal algorithmic superiority. The evidence chain demonstrates how an apparently strong benchmark result changes under progressively stricter assurance gates. Aggregate MAE concealed rare >100 mm errors; paired uncertainty moderated the headline result; ablation prevented attribution inflation; blind re-evaluation exposed split sensitivity; and OOD testing showed that in-distribution improvement did not imply resilience to changed noise regimes.

A production assurance program should therefore define explicit operating envelopes, fallback or abstention policies, independent datasets, and failure-oriented acceptance criteria before deployment.

## 6. Claim-control framework
Supported statements include: the public benchmark and source provenance were reproduced in a pinned computational chain; generic two-neighbour continuous interpolation reduced observed MAE on the original noise-free holdout; the magnitude of that improvement was not stable under the corrected internal blind re-split; the challenger exhibited material tail errors and degraded under the studied OOD noise condition; and ablation attributes the observed lift to generic interpolation rather than canonical NS-MDS.

This study does not support claims that NS-MDS caused the improvement, that the challenger is superior at conventional 95% confidence, that the method has independent external validation, that it has demonstrated operational-aircraft inspection performance, or that it is certified, regulator-approved, airline-approved, or ready for safety-critical deployment.

## 7. Limitations
The dataset is generated from finite-element simulations rather than a new independent experimental campaign. Primary and blind evaluations draw from the same underlying public noise-free dataset, so the blind gate tests split sensitivity rather than external generalization. Sample sizes are modest. The study does not evaluate probability-of-detection curves, sensor placement uncertainty, environmental/manufacturing variability, inspection-procedure effects, or human factors required for industrial qualification.

## 9. Conclusion
A strong average benchmark result is not equivalent to assured NDT performance. In this public guided-wave localization case, generic continuous interpolation reduced observed MAE from 15.000 to 8.668 mm on the original holdout, yet its paired 95% improvement interval crossed zero, errors above 100 mm occurred, OOD performance degraded, and the effect contracted to 3.98% under a corrected internal blind re-split. Evidence-first assurance should precede operational claims.
